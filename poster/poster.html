Do LLM judges of agent traces check the process,
or only the outcome?

A matched-pair experiment: the same trace, the same injected fault, differing only in whether the final answer is right.

Faizan
Trends in NLP · Univ. Trier

outcome-anchoring-llm-judges

1Hypothesis

LLM judges grade agents, and increasingly train them as reward signals. A judge scoring the final answer rather than the process rewards agents that fake their steps but land on the right number.

RQ. Do judges detect process faults regardless of the final outcome — or are they outcome-anchored?

H1 anchoring. Detection is lower when the final outcome is correct. NOT SUPPORTED
H2 fault type. Fabricated values and phantom actions are missed more than skipped checks. CONTRADICTED
H3 reasoning. Reason-then-verdict shrinks the gap. NOT SUPPORTED

Where this sits

  • TRAIL (Deshpande+ 2025), AgentRewardBench (Lù+ 2025): judges miss agent errors, but compare different traces — cause not isolated.
  • Lightman+ 2023: process vs outcome supervision. This work adds the counterfactual pair.

2Methodology

Environment. Expense reimbursement: the outcome is one number, so correct/wrong is unambiguous. 12 employees, 36 trips, 299 expense items, 4 policy rules, 6 deterministic tools. A Python oracle gives the true total.

Traces. A ReAct agent (Qwen3 14B) must reason in the open. A trace is kept only if its total matches the oracle and it passes a process audit: one run hit the oracle exactly while its four calculate calls held no arithmetic at all.

Faults — one per trace, at a recorded step:

F1fabricated value — states an amount no tool returned
F2phantom action — lookup deleted, its claims kept
F3skipped check — policy never read, rules still applied

Judges. 3 models × 2 readouts (direct Yes/No; reason-then-verdict) over 101 traces = 606 judgements, temperature 0. Judges never see the oracle.

42 matched pairs (14 per fault type) + 17 clean controls. A check verifies each pair is byte-identical before its closing lines, and that edited calculate calls still return what the real tool returns.

Judges do not verify. Llama 3.1 (direct) accepted all 101 traces; Qwen3 14B flagged 96% of faults and 82% of clean ones.

Outcome barely moves detection. Within a pair, trace and fault are identical. Columns shift little; rows differ enormously.

H1 not supported. Small gaps, disagreeing in sign. McNemar p > 0.12 throughout; 39% of detected faults localised to ±1 step.

Conclusion. Outcome anchoring is not the mechanism — the absence of verification is. What a judge reports is driven by model-specific response bias and prompt wording, not by the fault.
Limitations. 42 pairs per cell: intervals are wide, so a modest gap cannot be excluded — only a large one. Judges are 7–14B open models, not frontier systems. Agent yield was 17/36.
